| **Meeting summary** 
The team conducted the initial project kickoff meeting for the OPA 2 XNPA development in Eucland Township, Pennsylvania, following Steve Perkins' return from October leave. The project involves acquiring a strategically located industrial site with existing spec entitlements for three buildings, where Building B has already been constructed by current owner Portman. The team identified significant challenges including organized community opposition, traffic capacity restrictions enforced through private neighbor agreements, and complex approval pathways that may impact the target 2027 delivery timeline.
**Project Structure and Scope**
The development features three entitled buildings on a single tract, with Amazon targeting Building A for a 1.1 million square foot distribution center operation and converting Building C to trailer parking. The team selected MRP as the fee developer and plans to minimize changes to existing site plan approvals, focusing on interior modifications, associate parking reconfiguration, office space expansion, and security fencing. The project requires 435 associate parking stalls and approximately 100 dock doors for cross-dock operations.
**Community and Regulatory Challenges**
Eucland Township represents an affluent, historically rural community experiencing pressure from industrial development. Previous warehouse approvals generated significant opposition, with community meetings exceeding venue capacity when Amazon rumors circulated. The site operates under strict traffic caps enforced through private neighbor agreements, creating potential long-term operational constraints. Three specifically named neighbors hold private enforcement rights, though one daycare operation is no longer active.
**Traffic and Entitlement Analysis**
Preliminary traffic analysis indicates the operation would exceed entitled trip caps during seasonal peak periods by 10-15 trips in the PM peak window, while remaining compliant during non-seasonal operations. The site benefits from completed road infrastructure improvements including widened Cherry Boulevard and a new traffic signal, but faces hard truck route restrictions requiring westbound/eastbound flow only. Legal counsel Amy Farrell and Brielle Brown are reviewing all entitlement documents to identify required approvals and potential modification triggers.
**Site Acquisition Strategy**
The team faces a strategic decision between purchasing the entire site versus acquiring only Buildings A and C. Acquiring the full site provides operational simplicity and avoids subdivision complexities but requires purchasing Building B without immediate use plans. The alternative approach involves complex subdivision requirements, shared utility and stormwater easements, and potential indemnification agreements to protect against traffic violations by future Building B operators.
**Development Timeline and Next Steps**
The team acknowledged that even the simplest approval pathway challenges the 2027 delivery target, with 2028 representing a more realistic timeline. MRP will conduct a comprehensive review of existing documentation and produce an initial project schedule. The team will establish formal work orders, circulate traffic analysis data, and begin preliminary township discussions to chart the optimal approval pathway forward.
