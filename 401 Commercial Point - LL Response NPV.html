Observations

  • The comp is a 5-year term. Option A is 124 months and Option B is 186 months. Option A’s NER is $0.13 below the comp, and Option B’s is $0.08 above it.
  • Option A’s rent passes the comp’s year-5 rent ($7.75) in year 6. Option B’s passes it in year 8.
  • Over the first 120 months, Option B’s NPV net of TI is $4.1M lower than Option A’s. Option B runs 62 months longer.
  • Renewals: Landlord offers two 5-year options at fair market value, with a floor at the expiring rent and escalations no lower than the initial term. The options are void if Tenant assigns or subleases. Amazon’s LOI asked for two fixed 102% renewals plus three 95%-of-FMV renewals.
  • Management fee: Landlord proposes 2% of total net rent (base rent plus OpEx) instead of 1.5% of base rent. The difference is about $0.6M over the term for Option A and $0.9M for Option B, undiscounted.
  • Operating expenses: Landlord estimates $0.55 PSF plus the management fee and declines a cap on controllable operating expenses.
  • Late delivery: Landlord proposes liquidated damages to be negotiated in the lease, in place of the day-for-day rent credit.

Suggested Pushback

Each lever’s effect on NER, all else equal:

  1. Term discount. Both options price at roughly the comp’s NER for 2 to 3 times the comp’s term. Ask for NER below the comp that reflects the longer commitment. Examples:
    • 124 months, 6 months free, $6.35, 3.00% escalations: NER $6.10 (11% below the comp)
    • 186 months, 8 months free, $6.25, 3.00% escalations: NER $6.56 (4% below the comp)
  2. Free rent on Option A. Restore 6 months. Option B already has 6 months on the same starting-rent basis.
  3. Escalations on Option B. On the 15.5-year term, each 0.25% of escalation moves NER more than a $0.10 cut in starting rent does.
  4. Renewal options. Ask for at least one fixed-rate option. Tie the escalation floor to market rather than the initial term. Preserve the options for permitted transferees.
  5. Management fee. Base it on base rent only, at 1.5%.
  6. Controllable OpEx cap. Reinstate a cap, even if it is above 2%.
  7. TI allowance. Allow its use for Tenant improvements, subject to Landlord’s reasonable approval, not only for building improvements Landlord approves.
  8. Late delivery. Set the liquidated damages in the LOI, with day-for-day rent credit as the minimum, instead of leaving them to the lease.

Assumptions: Rent escalates on each 12-month anniversary starting in month 13, and free rent does not shift the escalation dates. Rent is paid monthly and discounted monthly at 7%. The TI allowance is received at the start. Operating expenses are held flat at Landlord’s $0.55 PSF estimate.
