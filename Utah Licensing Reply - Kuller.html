Happy to talk. I'm free [TIME] today. Here's the short version so you have it in front of you before we talk.

We are not licensed in Utah, and Utah has no exception that lets an out-of-state broker work a commercial deal alongside a local firm. Some states (Kansas, Kentucky, Wisconsin) let us file a cooperation agreement and work the deal. Utah doesn't. Utah law requires a Utah license to market, solicit buyers for, or negotiate the sale of Utah property, and that applies even if we're doing it from Arizona.

What Utah does allow: a Utah broker can split the commission with a broker licensed in another state. So the money works. The problem is the work itself.

That leaves two options:

  1. Referral. Colliers holds the listing, signs the listing agreement with the owner, collects the full commission, and pays us our share under a separate fee-sharing agreement between KBC and Colliers. The catch is that Colliers has to run the deal: OM, buyer outreach, tours, negotiations. We can't be on the OM as the contacts or be the ones calling buyers.
  2. Get our team licensed in Utah. If you want to actually run the sale, you (and anyone else on the team working it) would need nonresident Utah licenses under a Utah principal broker. Utah has no reciprocity, but because you're already licensed elsewhere, Utah can give you a shortened state-specific course and exam. That takes time, so it may not fit this assignment's timeline.

A few other points:

  • Under Utah law only a Utah-licensed broker can sue the owner for a commission. Our protection is the fee-sharing agreement with Colliers, so we need it signed before any work starts, with the split, timing of payment, and Ohio law/venue spelled out.
  • Calling it a "co-brokerage" or "cooperation" agreement doesn't change the analysis. If we're doing the marketing and negotiating, we're doing licensed work in Utah.
  • Until this is sorted out, please hold off on sending any marketing materials with KBC names or contact info on them.
