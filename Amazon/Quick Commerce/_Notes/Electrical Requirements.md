208 and 480 are both three phase/four wire systems and both should work for QC:

These are usually OK but require engineering to confirm:

200amps 208/120

200 amps 480/277

These are definitely OK and our preference

400 amps 208/120

400 amps 480/277

There are NOT OK

200 amps 120/240

400 amps 120/240

If we have 600 or 800 amps of 120/240 we may be ok, but that would be pretty rare to see.

Some more granular info, since its so important for our program needs and for the dorks amongst us (me being one of them)

**Single phase**/ is a three wire service and is shown as XXX amps, 120/240 volt.

**Three phase**/is a four wire service and is shown as XXX amps, 480/277 volt OR 208/120 volt

See diagram below.

While there is a difference between the 480 and 208, it is mainly in how the power is “stepped down” from the power lines into the building.  Both of those would suit our needs just fine.  If we’re being technical, 480 starts out with more power available but not something we should need to worry about.

The main thing we’re looking for is that our building, or office park complex, has 4 wires coming in (three phase power) from either the power pole or an underground electrical vault which would be considered “Three phase power”  three hot lines, one neutral.  Then we can work with it.

If its Single phase (which is technically three wires, 2 hots, one neutral) we will get tapped out and stuck unless its ~800 amps of single phase power.

See diagram below 😊

Those little barrels on the power poles are transformers and they step the power down from line voltage so its usable in a building.  In our case they take the three 7200 volt lines and step them down (Either to 480 or 208) and when there ARENT power lines, this all happens underground in power vaults.

The reason why a “power upgrade” can be such a deal breaker is because it means that the power company needs to evaluate how much power is coming out of those transformers and down into our building (or buildings if it serves multiples) and evaluate if there is enough capacity to increase the amperage to our specific site.  If there is not space in the existing transformer they might need to upgrade that transformer, which means a new service design, street closures, etc… the process is possible but can take over a year and be very costly.

So we look for buildings with adequate power available to avoid all of that.  Thankfully QC power requirements are pretty small, so unless we’re looking at stand alone older building in a non commercial area, we’ll typically be ok…
