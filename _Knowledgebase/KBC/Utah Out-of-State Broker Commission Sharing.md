# Can KBC (not Utah-licensed) get paid on a Utah sale, and what can it do?

**Short answer:** Getting paid is fine: a Utah broker may share a commission with a "principal broker of another jurisdiction." Doing the work is the problem. Utah has **no commercial cooperation carve-out**, so marketing, soliciting buyers, or negotiating a Utah sale without a Utah license is unlicensed activity, and calling the arrangement a "co-brokerage agreement" doesn't change that.

## Rules

- **Fee sharing is allowed.** Paying an unlicensed person is a disciplinary violation, *except* that consideration "may be shared with a principal broker of another jurisdiction." This exception is about **payment only**. It doesn't authorize the out-of-state broker to perform brokerage acts in Utah. (§ 61-2f-401(5)(a))
- **A license is required for brokerage acts.** For real estate located in Utah, you can't engage in, act in the capacity of, or *advertise or assume to act as* a broker or agent without a Utah license. One act for consideration of selling, or offering to sell, real estate for another person is enough to trigger it. (§ 61-2f-201(1)–(2))
- **There's no Utah equivalent of KS/KY/NH/WI-style commercial cooperation statutes.** Nothing in 61-2f or R162-2f lets an out-of-state commercial broker work a deal alongside a Utah broker under a filed agreement.
- **The right to sue for a fee is restricted.** You can't sue in a Utah court for compensation for any act the chapter prohibits. Only principal brokers, or entities affiliated with one on Division records, can sue. KBC can't sue the owner in Utah. Its fee claim runs against the Utah broker under the fee-sharing agreement, so put choice-of-law and forum (Ohio) clauses in that agreement. (§ 61-2f-409)
- **Advertising.** A Utah licensee's ads must "clearly and conspicuously" identify the licensee's brokerage, with the name as registered with the Division. Teams and marketing groups are treated the same as individuals. The rule does **not** require any brokerage to be "most prominent," and it doesn't prescribe any "licensed in X only / in cooperation with" disclaimer. (R162-2f-401h)
- **Getting licensed.** Utah has no general reciprocity. The Division *may* enter reciprocal agreements, but none was confirmed for KBC's states. A nonresident can be licensed. A nonresident associate broker or sales agent must be affiliated with a **Utah-licensed principal broker**, who can be a nonresident. Applicants who already hold an out-of-state broker license may get a reduced state-specific course and exam. (§ 61-2f-203(1)(d)(ii)(B), (1)(e)(ii), (3), (4))

## Structuring paths

| Path | What KBC can do | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Pure referral** | Nothing in Utah. No OM marketing, buyer outreach, tours, or negotiation | Utah broker signs the listing agreement and does all the work. A KBC ↔ Utah broker referral agreement sets the split. § 61-2f-401(5)(a) |
| **Utah broker of record + KBC team licensed in Utah** | Full capital-markets execution | Team members get nonresident associate broker or agent licenses under a Utah principal broker: either the partner firm or a KBC Utah principal broker. § 61-2f-203(3) |
| **"Co-brokerage" with KBC actively marketing and negotiating, unlicensed** | 🚩 Not supported | No statutory carve-out. Risks: unlicensed activity, an unenforceable fee, and discipline exposure for the Utah broker under § 61-2f-401(5) and its supervisory duties |

🚩 **Unsettled:** Utah law doesn't say whether purely out-of-state activity, such as calling out-of-state buyers about a Utah asset from Ohio, counts as acting "with respect to real estate located in this state." Assume it does, because the statute keys on the property's location, not the broker's. Confirm with Utah counsel or the Division before relying on anything narrower.
