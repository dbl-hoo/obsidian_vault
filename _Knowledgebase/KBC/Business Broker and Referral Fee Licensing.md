# Business Broker and Referral Fee Licensing — State Guide

*Researched 2026-06-17. Sources: IBBA (Apr 2025), ehowardlaw.com, totalbusinessbrokers.com.*

## Key Distinction

**"Business broker" licensing** applies to brokering the **sale or purchase of a business** — not to referral or finder's fee arrangements for service introductions. If KBC is referring customers to a third party for its *services* (not selling the business), the regulated "business broker" category likely does not apply.

## Service Referral / Finder's Fee Arrangements

For pure service referral fees (introducing customers to a company for its services):

- Generally **no state licensing requirement** — most states do not regulate commercial service referral arrangements.
- **Illinois** — broad SOS registration language; worth a specific check if the counterparty is IL-based.
- **CA, FL** — scope language is broad enough to confirm, but service-only referrals are generally outside their business broker statutes.
- RE license creates no hook unless the referral is RE-related.

## Checklist Before Taking a Service Referral Fee

1. Confirm the arrangement is for **services**, not a business sale or equity transaction.
2. Get a **written finder's fee agreement** — define trigger event, fee amount/structure, payment timing, and term.
3. If counterparty or referred customers are in **IL, CA, or FL**, do a quick scope check on the applicable statute.
4. If any referred customers are also **KBC RE clients**, disclose the dual relationship and get consent.
5. Check **KBC internal policy** on principals receiving outside referral income.
6. Referral income is **ordinary income** — track and report for tax.
