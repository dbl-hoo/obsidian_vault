# Does Delaware require specific disclosures or provisions in a commercial listing agreement?

**Short answer:** Modest requirements. Listing agreements must be in writing and signed, the broker has to deliver copies, and compensation depends on a written agreement. The one mandatory disclosure sentence for commercial deals sits in the *agreement of sale or lease* (24 Del. C. § 2938(f)), not the listing agreement. No CIS is needed for commercial. KBC is licensed in DE.

## Rules

- **Written brokerage agreement needed to earn a fee.** A buyer, tenant, seller or landlord owes no compensation to a broker without a written brokerage agreement. 24 Del. C. § 2930.
  - If a lease turns into a sale, the broker gets paid on the sale only if the listing agreement provided for compensation on a later sale. Draft for it.
  - Brokers may cooperate with and compensate other brokers without further permission.
- **Listing agreements must be in writing and signed.** Sale, lease or exchange, exclusive or non-exclusive, signed by the seller/owner and the broker or the broker's designee. 24 Del. Admin. Code § 2900-8.0.
- **Copies.** Every party to a listing agreement, agreement of sale, buyer agency agreement or lease gets a copy when they sign or initial it, and a fully executed copy after everyone has signed. The licensee delivers the executed copy within a reasonable time. 24 Del. Admin. Code § 2900-8.0.
- **Agency disclosure.** Common law agents must disclose in writing whom they represent (§ 2932(c)(1)). For a written confirmation of the agency relationship, see the agreement-of-sale/lease item below.
- **No CIS for commercial.** The Consumer Information Statement is for 1-4 family residential. For non-residential property, § 2938(f) replaces it with this language in the agreement of sale or lease, placed after the confirmation of the agency relationships:
  > "The parties acknowledge that they have certain rights and responsibilities under Delaware agency law (Title 24 of the Delaware Code, Chapter 29) and may consult with their legal counsel."
  - Practical point: put the same sentence in the listing agreement too, but the statute places the requirement on the sale/lease agreement.
- **Confidentiality duties** start at first contact (§ 2938(d)). Other duties start at the first appointment, first showing or offer (§ 2934).
- **Not applicable to commercial:** CMA "not an appraisal" warning (§ 2931) applies only when a CMA is used. Statutory-agency default is for 1-4 family residential.

## Not verified — 🚩 confirm before relying

- Rule 2900-8.0 has other subsections (expiration dates, advertising, holdover, net listings, etc.). I confirmed the written/signature and copy provisions but did not pull the full text of the rest. Read the full rule before drafting.
- Statute text was pulled through a web summarizer, not read directly. The § 2938(f) quote and the § 2932/2933 cites should be checked against the official code before quoting to a client.
- No Delaware statutory rule on a mandatory expiration date or protection period for commercial listings found.
