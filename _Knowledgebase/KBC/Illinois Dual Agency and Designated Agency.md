# Illinois — one firm on both sides of a lease: dual agency vs. designated agency

**Short answer:** If *different* KBC licensees represent the landlord and the tenant, Illinois treats it as designated agency (225 ILCS 454/15-50) — not dual agency, no dual-agency consent required, but each brokerage agreement must specifically name that client's designated agents. If the *same* licensee works both sides, it's statutory dual agency (225 ILCS 454/15-45) requiring informed written consent from **both** clients.

## The two regimes

**Designated agency — 225 ILCS 454/15-50**
- The sponsoring broker (KBC) is *not* considered to act for more than one party if its brokerage agreements specifically designate the licensees acting as each client's legal agents, and those designated licensees don't represent more than one party.
- Dual agency is measured at the level of the *designated agent*, not the firm.
- Compliance hook: the written brokerage agreement (listing agreement, tenant rep agreement) must name the designated licensees. An agreement silent on designation is a gap.

**Dual agency — 225 ILCS 454/15-45**
- Triggered only when the same licensee(s) represent both landlord and tenant.
- Requires **informed written consent of all clients**, two steps:
  1. Statutory dual-agency disclosure form (conflict-of-interest explanation, what may/may not be disclosed) presented **at the time the brokerage agreement is entered into**, signed before the licensee acts as dual agent.
  2. Written **confirmation** of consent at the time the client executes any offer or lease — may live inside the lease/offer document but must be separately signed *and initialed*.
- **The form language is statutory** — 15-45 prescribes the text of both documents ("substantially the following"). Disclosure form: conflict warning + enumerated can-do / cannot-disclose lists. Confirmation: *"The undersigned confirm that they have previously consented to [name], acting as a Dual Agent... and specifically consent to Licensee acting as a Dual Agent in regard to the transaction referred to in this document."* Track the statutory text; don't redraft it.
- Prohibited entirely where the licensee (or an entity the licensee owns an interest in) is a party to the transaction. **By rule, this reaches indirect ownership:** 68 Ill. Adm. Code 1450.820 bars dual agency when the licensee or an entity in which the licensee has or will have any ownership interest, *"directly or indirectly,"* is a party. No de minimis exception — a minority stake in an entity that holds a minority stake in a party still triggers the bar. No consent form cures it; restructure to designated agency with a licensee from outside the ownership group.

## Practical checklist (firm on both sides of an IL lease)

1. Confirm who the designated agents are on each side. Different people → designated agency path.
2. Check both brokerage agreements name the designated licensees per 15-50; amend if silent.
3. Any licensee overlap between the two teams → first check the 1450.820 ownership bar (any direct *or indirect* interest in a party kills dual agency outright); if clear, run the full 15-45 consent process with both clients (disclosure form + confirmation at lease execution).
4. Even on the designated-agency path, a written acknowledgment from both clients that KBC represents the other side is cheap belt-and-suspenders and heads off later disputes.
