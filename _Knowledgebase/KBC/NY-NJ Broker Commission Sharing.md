# Can a NY Broker Share a Commission with an NJ Broker?

**Short answer: Yes.** NY RPL § 442 expressly permits a NY broker to split a commission with "a person regularly engaged in the real estate brokerage business in a state outside of New York" — a licensed NJ broker qualifies.

## NY → NJ (NY broker paying the NJ broker)

- **Basis:** N.Y. Real Property Law § 442 (Splitting commissions). The out-of-state-broker exception applies to both general commission splits and original-sale co-op/condo commissions.
- **Caveat:** The NJ broker may not perform license-required brokerage activity **inside New York** (physically showing, negotiating, soliciting in-state). NY has no reciprocity shortcut for in-state activity — the NJ broker would need a NY nonresident license to actually work the deal in NY.
- **Structure:** NJ broker works from/in NJ (referral, out-of-state client rep); NY licensee handles all NY-side activity. Paper the split with a written co-broker or referral agreement.

## NJ → NY (mirror image — NJ broker paying the NY broker)

- **Basis:** N.J.S.A. 45:15-3.1. NJ is stricter: an NJ broker may cooperate with and compensate an out-of-state broker **only if** the out-of-state broker conducts no negotiations within New Jersey.
- Same structural fix: keep the out-of-state broker's activity outside NJ; NJ licensee does all in-state work.
