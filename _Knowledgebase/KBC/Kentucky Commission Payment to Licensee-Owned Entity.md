# Kentucky — Can a licensee direct commissions to an LLC the licensee owns?

**Short answer:** No. Kentucky has no PLLC/entity carve-out. KRS 324.020(4) bars a broker from splitting fees with or compensating *any person* not licensed under Chapter 324 (and "person" includes LLCs under KRS 446.010(33)); KRS 324.160(4)(f) independently bars the licensee from accepting compensation from anyone except their principal broker under a compensation agreement. Kentucky licenses only individuals — there is no entity/company license the LLC could obtain — so paying the LLC has no lawful path. 🚩 Confirm with KREC or KY counsel before telling a licensee "no" in writing.

## The rule

**Broker side — KRS 324.020(4):** "No broker shall split fees with or compensate any person who is not licensed to perform any of the acts regulated by this chapter," with only three exceptions:

- (a) referral fee to a **broker licensed outside Kentucky** for referring a client to the KY broker;
- (b) commission/compensation to an **out-of-state broker** complying with KRS 324.235–324.238 (the commercial cooperation path);
- (c) payment to a **licensed auctioneer** collaborating on a real estate auction.

A licensee's own LLC is none of those. Contrast states with an express "licensed entity" or PLLC route — Kentucky's exception list is closed and does not include one.

**Licensee side — KRS 324.160(4)(f):** sanctionable to accept "valuable consideration for the performance of any of the acts specified in this chapter, from any person, except from his or her principal broker in accordance with a compensation agreement between them." Even if KBC were willing to route the check, the *licensee* takes the hit: the money must move principal broker → licensee, not principal broker → licensee's LLC.

**"Person" includes the LLC:** KRS 446.010(33) extends "person" to "bodies-politic and corporate, societies, communities, the public generally, individuals, partnerships, joint stock companies, and limited liability companies." So the LLC is a separate unlicensed person for KRS 324.020(4) purposes — not a transparent alter ego of the licensee.

**No way to license the LLC:** KRS Chapter 324 licenses individuals (broker under KRS 324.046(1), sales associate under 324.046(2), principal broker per KRS 324.010(4)). There is no corporate/LLC license; a brokerage "company" exists only as the principal broker's registered firm name/office (KRS 324.330 name-change notice; 201 KAR 11:210 Form 202). An agent cannot register their own single-member LLC as a KY brokerage without that LLC having its own principal broker, office, and KREC registration — i.e., it becomes a real competing brokerage, not a pay-through vehicle.

## Practical guidance

- **Answer the licensee:** commissions go to the licensee personally under the KBC compensation agreement. KBC cuts the check to the individual; what the licensee does with the money afterward (contributing it to their own LLC, S-corp election on personal returns, etc.) is their tax problem, not a brokerage-payment problem — but KBC should not be the payor to the entity and should not issue the 1099 to the LLC's EIN for KY-earned brokerage fees.
- **Don't paper around it.** A side letter, assignment of commission, or "pay to the order of" instruction doesn't fix KRS 324.020(4) — the statute reaches compensation, not just the form of the payee line. Both KBC's KY principal broker and the licensee are exposed (KRS 324.160(1) sanctions: suspension, revocation, fine up to $1,000, probation, reprimand; KRS 324.990 for unlicensed activity).
- **If the licensee is an out-of-state broker**, KRS 324.020(4)(a)–(b) may allow payment — but to the *licensed out-of-state broker*, which can be an entity if that entity holds the license in its home state. That's a different question from an unlicensed LLC set up for tax purposes. See [[Out-of-State Commission Paths]].
- 🚩 **Unsettled edge:** no KREC declaratory ruling or published KY case squarely addressing a single-member LLC owned by the licensee surfaced in this research. The statutory text is plain, but if the licensee pushes, get a written KREC staff position (ppc.krec@ky.gov) rather than relying on this summary.
