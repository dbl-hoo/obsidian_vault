# North Carolina — Statutory Disclosure & Advertising Requirements for Real Estate Brokers

**Short answer:** NC requires (1) an agency-disclosure conversation + the "Working With Real Estate Agents" (WWREA) publication at "first substantial contact" — commercial deals are **not** exempt — and (2) firm name (not necessarily individual name) on every ad, in the ad content itself, no exceptions for social media character limits or team/trade names. Both apply to KBC now that it's directly licensed in NC.

## Agency Disclosure — 21 NCAC 58A .0104

- At **"first substantial contact"** with a prospective buyer or seller, a broker must: give them a copy of the WWREA publication, put the broker's name and license number on it, review it with them, and determine which agency role the broker will fill (seller's agent, buyer's agent, or dual agent).
- **"First substantial contact"** = the point where the consumer begins sharing confidential/personal information or the relationship starts acting like an agency relationship — not merely a first phone call or a name exchange.
- **Phone/electronic first contact exception:** if it's impractical to hand over the publication in person, the broker must transmit it **within 3 days** of first substantial contact and review it at the earliest practicable opportunity after that.
- **No commercial exemption** — NCREC made the WWREA form mandatory for **both commercial and residential** sales transactions effective 7/1/2021. Treat every NC deal (industrial, commercial, whatever) as subject to this.
- Dual agency requires **written consent of all parties** (N.C.G.S. § 93A-6(a)(4)) — undisclosed dual representation is a licensing violation.
- General duty to discover and disclose **material facts** — N.C.G.S. § 93A-6(a)(1); 21 NCAC 58A .0114(c). Any fact that could affect a reasonable party's decision to transact. Best practice: put the disclosure in writing and keep it in the file for 3 years.

## Advertising — 21 NCAC 58A .0105

- Every ad — sign, business card, website, social post, anything — must include the **firm name** (or sole-proprietorship name) the broker is affiliated with. Individual broker name is optional; firm name is not.
- **No "one-click" exception.** The firm name has to be visible in the ad content itself — a bio/profile link or landing-page mention doesn't satisfy the rule. This matters for LinkedIn/social posts specifically.
- **Blind ads prohibited** — can't advertise in a way that makes a property look owner-sold (i.e., hiding that a broker is involved).
- Broker-in-charge sign-off is expected before ads run.
- **Team/trade names:** a name other than the broker's or firm's legal name (N.C.G.S. § 93A-2(c)) must be registered with the county and NCREC must be notified. Team ads must still carry the actual registered firm name prominently — a team name can't stand in for it, and terms like "realty," "real estate," "properties," or "company" in a team name are red flags for implying an independent brokerage when the team operates under KBC's firm license.
- Violations run from reprimand/censure up through suspension or revocation, and willful violations can be charged as a Class 1 misdemeanor under N.C.G.S. § 93A-8.

### Can KBC advertise as "KBC Advisors" instead of the licensed entity name?

**Short answer: not by default.** Rule .0105 requires the ad to show "the name of the firm... with which the broker is affiliated" — i.e., the licensed name. KBC's NC-licensed entity is **KBC Advisors Services, LLC**, so that's what has to appear unless "KBC Advisors" is separately registered as an assumed/trade name.

- To advertise under "KBC Advisors" (dropping "Services, LLC"), KBC must:
  1. File an assumed business name certificate for "KBC Advisors" under the NC Assumed Business Names Act, **G.S. § 66-71.4** — filing is still done at the **county Register of Deeds** (each county where KBC will do business under the name), not with the Secretary of State. The 2017 Act just added a statewide searchable index on top of the county filings — it didn't move the filing itself to the state level.
  2. Separately **notify NCREC in writing** of the assumed name, per **21 NCAC 58A .0103(c)**. County/state filing alone isn't enough — NCREC has to have it on file too.
  3. Keep the filing current — update within 60 days of any change.
  - Search existing statewide assumed-name filings at `sosnc.gov/abn/search` before filing, to confirm "KBC Advisors" isn't already taken/conflicting.
- Until both steps are done, ads for NC deals should carry the full licensed name, **KBC Advisors Services, LLC** (a shortened display like "KBC Advisors Services" alongside it is fine — the risk is dropping "Services"/"LLC" entirely without the assumed-name filing backing it up).
- 🚩 Confirm with local NC counsel or NCREC directly before running ads under the shortened name — this determines whether a filing is strictly required or whether NCREC's practice tolerates a good-faith abbreviation; the primary rule text (.0105/.0103(c)) wasn't independently re-verified against the current NCAC PDF for this specific point (see caveat below).

## What This Means for KBC in NC

- Any NC broker on a KBC deal needs to run the WWREA disclosure at first substantial contact — commercial deals included, don't assume it's a residential-only form.
- Marketing flyers, LOIs cover pages, listing sites, and any social posts for NC properties need **"KBC Advisors Services, LLC"** (the actual licensed name) visible in the ad itself, not just a footer link or bio — unless/until "KBC Advisors" is filed as an assumed name (see above).
- If any NC broker operates under a sub-brand/team name, that name needs county + NCREC registration and the actual KBC firm name still has to appear prominently in ads.
