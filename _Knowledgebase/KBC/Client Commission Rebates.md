# Can a broker rebate part of its commission to its own client?

State-by-state. Add states as they come up.

## New Hampshire — 🚩 NO (cash rebate to a principal is prohibited)

**Short answer:** A NH licensee **cannot** pay any part of a commission to a principal (buyer, seller, tenant, landlord) who isn't licensed. The Commission ruled on this exact question in 2018. A broker **may** lower the commission it charges a principal.

- **Statute:** RSA 331-A:26, XXIV makes it prohibited conduct to pay or offer to pay "valuable consideration" to anyone not licensed under the chapter. The only exceptions are (1) out-of-state licensed brokers and (2) entities under RSA 331-A:16-b. There is no exception for clients.
- **"Valuable consideration"** is anything worth more than $100 (Rea 101.01(i)). Gifts of $100 or less fall outside the rule.
- **Declaratory ruling (June 19, 2018), "Whether Non-Licensed Principals Can Share Transaction Commissions":** "such a sharing is not allowed." The property-owner exemption from licensure (RSA 331-A:4, I) doesn't help, because it exempts the owner but leaves the licensee's own duties in place. Under *Kowalski v. Cedars of Portsmouth Condominium*, 146 N.H. 130 (2001), agreements to pay commissions to non-licensees are void. RSA 331-A:32 also bars non-licensees from suing for compensation.
- **The ruling's carve-out:** "There is nothing, however, that prohibits a broker from lowering the commission to be charged to a principal to the transaction."
- **Disclosure, separately:** RSA 331-A:26, XIII bars any *undisclosed* commission, rebate, or profit taken on the principal's expenditures.
- **Still current:** the current text of RSA 331-A:26 (last amended 2026, 21:7-9, eff. June 2, 2026) still contains the XXIV prohibition. No later ruling or statute reversing the 2018 ruling turned up.
- **Not relevant:** the Commission's "Concessions" ruling deals with whether concessions are *confidential*, not with rebates. RSA 402:39 is an *insurance* anti-rebating statute.

**Structuring (needs local counsel to confirm):**
- If KBC's own client pays the fee, the clean route is a reduced fee, which the ruling expressly allows.
- If the landlord or seller pays the fee and the client is the tenant (e.g., an Amazon rebate under the Amazon commission waterfall), a post-closing check from the broker to the client is the prohibited act.
- Possible alternative: KBC accepts a reduced commission from the landlord, and the landlord passes the difference to the tenant as a concession (rent credit or TI) negotiated between the principals. This is untested in NH. 🚩 Confirm with NH counsel before any deal relies on it.
- Out-of-state angle: if KBC's share flows through an NH cooperating broker (RSA 331-A:22-a), that broker is a NH licensee bound by XXIV. See [[Out-of-State Commission Paths]].
