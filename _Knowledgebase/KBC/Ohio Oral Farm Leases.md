# Ohio Oral Farm Leases — ORC 5301.71

Effective July 21, 2022 (HB 397). Codified at R.C. § 5301.71.

> **This is research, not a legal opinion.** Confirm current text before advising a client.

## What It Covers

Applies to any agricultural lease agreement — written **or oral** — that lacks a termination date or a deadline for notice of termination. Covers crop leases (planting, growing, harvesting).

**Excluded:** pasture leases, timber leases, farm building leases, horticultural building leases, equipment-only leases.

## The Core Rule — September 1 Deadline

A **landlord** who wants to terminate must deliver **written notice** to the tenant **on or before September 1** of the year the termination is to take effect.

**Permitted delivery methods:** personal delivery, fax, or email.

**Notice should include:**
- Date of the notice
- Identity of the leased property
- Termination date

## When the Lease Ends

After timely notice, the lease terminates on the **earlier** of:
1. The date harvesting/removal of crops is complete; or
2. December 31 of the year notice was given

— unless the parties agree otherwise **in writing**.

## Holdover / Late Notice Consequence

If the landlord misses the September 1 deadline, **the lease automatically continues for another full lease term.** The tenant can either force continuation or seek damages.

## Tenant's Position

The September 1 deadline only binds **landlords** — tenants are not subject to it. A tenant can invoke the holdover protection against a landlord who tries to terminate after the deadline.

## Practical Notes

- Oral "handshake" farm leases are extremely common in Ohio and are fully governed by this statute.
- Without a written lease containing its own termination provisions, the landlord is locked into the September 1 deadline every year.
- **Recommendation:** Written leases with explicit termination provisions and notice deadlines avoid the ambiguity entirely.
